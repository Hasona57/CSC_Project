Exercise 1: Moment of a Force

Task: A force of F = 100 N is applied at an angle of 30° above the horizontal to the end of a horizontal lever arm of length d = 2.0 m attached to a pivot at point O. Calculate the magnitude of the moment about point O.

Formula to use: M = Fy × d = (F · sin(θ)) × d

Click to view Solution & Explanation

Step-by-Step Solution:

Fx = 100 * cos(30°) = 86.6 N  (passes through pivot O, so moment arm = 0)
Fy = 100 * sin(30°) = 50.0 N  (perpendicular to the 2.0 m lever arm)

Mo = Fy * d
Mo = 50.0 N * 2.0 m = 100 N·m (counter-clockwise)

Explanation:

  • Varignon's Theorem: Instead of finding the perpendicular distance to the angled line of action, we split the force into horizontal (Fx) and vertical (Fy) components.
  • Line of Action: Since Fx passes directly through point O, it produces zero moment, leaving only Fy × d.

Exercise 2: Rigid Body with Pin and Cable

Task: A horizontal rigid beam of length 4 m is attached to a wall by a pin at Point A. The other end, Point B, is supported by a cable that connects to the wall, making a 30° angle with the beam. A downward load of 1000 N rests on the beam at a distance of 3 m from the pin. Determine the tension (T) in the cable and the horizontal and vertical reactions at the pin (Ax and Ay).

Formulas to use: ΣMA = 0, ΣFx = 0, and ΣFy = 0

Click to view Solution & Explanation

Step-by-Step Solution:


1. Take moments about the pin at Point A (ΣMA = 0):
   -(1000 N * 3 m) + (Ty * 4 m) = 0
   -3000 + (T * sin(30°) * 4) = 0
   -3000 + (T * 0.5 * 4) = 0
   2 * T = 3000
   T = 1500 N (Tension in the cable)

2. Sum horizontal forces (ΣFx = 0):
   Ax - Tx = 0
   Ax - (T * cos(30°)) = 0
   Ax - (1500 * 0.866) = 0
   Ax = 1299 N (acting to the right)

3. Sum vertical forces (ΣFy = 0):
   Ay + Ty - 1000 = 0
   Ay + (1500 * 0.5) - 1000 = 0
   Ay + 750 - 1000 = 0
   Ay = 250 N (acting upward)

Explanation:

  • Decomposing the Tension: The cable pulls at an angle, so we split it into a vertical part (Ty = T * sin(30°)) that holds the beam up, and a horizontal part (Tx = T * cos(30°)) that pushes the beam into the wall.
  • Moments first: Taking moments around the pin at A is the best first step because the unknown pin forces (Ax and Ay) pass straight through it and create zero moment. This leaves T as the only unknown in the first equation.
  • Pin Reactions: Unlike a simple roller, a pin support resists movement in both directions, which is why we must solve for both Ax and Ay using the X and Y force equations.
