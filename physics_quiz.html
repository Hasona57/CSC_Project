Lecture 1: Dimensions and Units

Flashcards

1. SI Base Quantities and Units

Length: meter (m)
Mass: kilogram (kg)
Time: second (s)
Temperature: kelvin (K)
Electric current: ampere (A)
Amount of substance: mole (mol)
Luminous intensity: candela (cd)

2. Dimensions of Basic Quantities

Length $= [L]$, Mass $= [M]$, Time $= [T]$. Every mechanical quantity can be written as a combination of these three dimensions.

3. Dimensions of Common Derived Quantities

Velocity: $[LT^{-1}]$, Acceleration: $[LT^{-2}]$, Force: $[MLT^{-2}]$,
Energy / Work: $[ML^2T^{-2}]$, Power: $[ML^2T^{-3}]$, Pressure: $[ML^{-1}T^{-2}]$, Density: $[ML^{-3}]$.

4. Dimensional Homogeneity

Every term in a physically correct equation must have the same dimensions. Only quantities with the same dimensions can be added or subtracted. Arguments of trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions must be dimensionless.

5. Uses and Limits of Dimensional Analysis

Uses: check the correctness of an equation, derive the form of a relation between quantities, and convert between unit systems.
Limits: it cannot determine dimensionless constants (such as $2\pi$ or $\tfrac{1}{2}$) and cannot handle sums of different functions.

6. Unit Prefixes and Conversion

giga (G) $= 10^{9}$, mega (M) $= 10^{6}$, kilo (k) $= 10^{3}$, milli (m) $= 10^{-3}$, micro ($\mu$) $= 10^{-6}$, nano (n) $= 10^{-9}$.
To convert units, multiply by conversion factors equal to 1 (e.g. $1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m}$) so the unwanted units cancel.

Lecture 2: Elasticity

Flashcards

1. Stress

Force per unit area: $\sigma = \frac{F}{A}$, measured in pascals (Pa = N/m²). Can be tensile, compressive, or shear stress.

2. Strain

Fractional deformation: $\varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0}$. Strain is dimensionless (no units).

3. Hooke's Law and Young's Modulus

Within the elastic limit, stress is proportional to strain: $Y = \frac{F/A}{\Delta L / L_0}$, so $\Delta L = \frac{F L_0}{A Y}$. Units: Pa.

4. Shear Modulus

$S = \frac{F/A}{\Delta x / h} = \frac{\text{shear stress}}{\text{shear strain}}$, where $\Delta x / h = \tan\theta \approx \theta$ for small angles.

5. Bulk Modulus

$B = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V / V_0}$. The negative sign shows volume decreases as pressure increases. Compressibility is $k = \frac{1}{B}$.

6. Stress-Strain Curve and Elastic Energy

Key points: proportional limit → elastic limit → yield point → ultimate strength → fracture point.
Elastic energy density: $\frac{U}{V} = \frac{1}{2}\sigma\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} Y \varepsilon^2$.

Lecture 3: Fluid Statics

Flashcards

1. Density and Specific Gravity

Density: $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$ (kg/m³). Specific gravity: $SG = \frac{\rho}{\rho_{water}}$, where $\rho_{water} = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$. SG has no units.

2. Pressure

$P = \frac{F}{A}$, measured in pascals (Pa). Standard atmosphere: $1\ \text{atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$.

3. Pressure Variation with Depth

$P = P_0 + \rho g h$. The term $\rho g h$ is the gauge pressure. Pressure is the same at all points at the same depth in a connected static fluid.

4. Pascal's Principle

A pressure change applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid. Hydraulic lift: $\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}$.

5. Archimedes' Principle and Buoyancy

Buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced: $B = \rho_{fluid} V_{displaced}\, g$. A floating object has $\frac{V_{submerged}}{V_{object}} = \frac{\rho_{object}}{\rho_{fluid}}$.

6. Measuring Pressure

Barometer: $P_{atm} = \rho g h$ (mercury column of about 760 mm at 1 atm).
Open-tube manometer: measures gauge pressure from the height difference of the liquid in the two arms.

Lecture 4: Fluid Dynamics

Flashcards

1. Ideal Fluid Assumptions

An ideal fluid is incompressible, non-viscous, flows in a steady (laminar) manner, and is irrotational.

2. Equation of Continuity

$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$ (conservation of mass for an incompressible fluid). Volume flow rate: $Q = A v$ (m³/s).

3. Bernoulli's Equation

Along a streamline: $P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g y = \text{constant}$. It expresses conservation of energy for a flowing fluid.

4. Torricelli's Theorem

Speed of efflux from a hole at depth $h$ below the open surface of a tank: $v = \sqrt{2 g h}$.

5. Viscosity, Poiseuille's Law, and Stokes' Law

Poiseuille's law: $Q = \frac{\pi r^4 \Delta P}{8 \eta L}$.
Stokes' law (drag on a sphere): $F = 6\pi \eta r v$.
Viscosity $\eta$ is measured in Pa·s.

6. Reynolds Number

$Re = \frac{\rho v D}{\eta}$. Roughly, $Re < 2000$ means laminar flow and $Re > 4000$ means turbulent flow, with a transition region in between.

Lecture 5: Oscillatory Motion

Flashcards

1. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

Motion under a restoring force proportional to displacement: $F = -kx$, giving $a = -\omega^2 x$.

2. Position, Velocity, and Acceleration in SHM

$x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$, $v = -A\omega\sin(\omega t + \phi)$, $a = -A\omega^2\cos(\omega t + \phi)$.
Maximum values: $v_{max} = A\omega$ and $a_{max} = A\omega^2$.

3. Frequency, Period, and the Mass-Spring System

$\omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T}$. For a mass on a spring: $\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$ and $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$.

4. Simple Pendulum

For small angles (about $15^\circ$ or less): $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$. The period does not depend on the mass or amplitude.

5. Energy in SHM

Total energy: $E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2$ (constant). Kinetic energy: $K = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2 - x^2)$. Speed: $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$.

6. Damped and Forced Oscillations, Resonance

Damped: amplitude decays, $x = A e^{-bt/2m}\cos(\omega' t + \phi)$.
Resonance: large amplitude occurs when the driving frequency is close to the natural frequency of the system.

Lecture 6: Waves

Flashcards

1. Types of Waves

Transverse: particles move perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.
Longitudinal: particles move parallel to the direction of wave travel (e.g. sound).
Mechanical waves need a medium; electromagnetic waves do not.

2. Wave Speed

$v = f\lambda$. For a string under tension $T$ with mass per unit length $\mu$: $v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$.

3. Traveling Wave Function

$y(x,t) = A\sin(kx - \omega t)$, where $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$, $\omega = 2\pi f$, and $v = \frac{\omega}{k}$.

4. Superposition and Interference

Constructive: path difference $= n\lambda$.
Destructive: path difference $= (n + \tfrac{1}{2})\lambda$.

5. Standing Waves on a String

Fixed at both ends: $\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}$ and $f_n = \frac{n v}{2L}$, for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ Nodes have zero displacement; antinodes have maximum displacement.

6. Sound Waves and the Doppler Effect

Speed of sound in air at $20^\circ$C is about $343\ \text{m/s}$.
Doppler effect: $f' = f\,\frac{v \pm v_{o}}{v \mp v_{s}}$, using the upper sign when the observer moves toward the source or the source moves toward the observer.
